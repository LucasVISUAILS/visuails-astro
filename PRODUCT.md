# Product

## Register

brand

## Platform

web

## Users

Established clothing and e-commerce brands that currently book photoshoots. They already spend real money on production days — a photographer, a studio, a model, styling, retouching, and their own day on set — and they already have a launch calendar they have to hit. They arrive sceptical rather than curious: the objection is not price, it is whether AI-made imagery will look cheap next to what their photographer delivers. They are evaluating a replacement for something that works but is slow and expensive, not shopping for a novelty.

Their job to be done is getting a whole collection photographed, finished and live before a launch date, without the production day and without the three-week turnaround.

## Product Purpose

VISUAILS produces finished product visuals for a brand's entire drop — catalog sets, lifestyle carousels and short video — from photos the brand already has, delivered inside 48 hours and reviewed by a human before it ships. The site exists to convert a brand that is about to book a shoot day into a brand that books a drop instead. Success is a drop ordered through `/start` with a delivery date the capacity gate has actually cleared, and a brand that comes back for the next collection.

## Positioning

The drop is the unit of sale: one collection, one deadline, one invoice — priced and judged against the photoshoot it replaces, not against an AI tool.

## Conversion & proof

- Primary CTA: the €0.99 test sample — one product, turned into a publish-ready visual, sitewide.
- Secondary CTA: `/start` — begin a drop, capacity-gated, no obligation until the deposit.
- The line a visitor remembers after 10 seconds: **One drop. One deadline. One invoice.**
- Belief ladder, in order:
  1. This is not an AI toy — the output is real, finished and human-reviewed.
  2. It holds up across a whole collection, not just one lucky frame.
  3. It costs less and lands sooner than the shoot day it replaces.
  4. Finding out costs €0.99.
- Proof on hand: finished work only, with no client named. Real output can be shown as imagery; no testimonials, no logos, no client names, no performance figures are available to publish. The "No wall of reviews yet" section carries this honestly, industry figures are labelled as industry figures, and the three case-study slots stay empty until there is something true to put in them.

## Brand Personality

Precise, exact, engineered. A visitor should feel they are looking at an instrument, not a service page — that the confidence on display comes from visible control rather than from claims. The voice states numbers and constraints plainly, admits what it does not have yet, and never reaches for enthusiasm to fill a gap. Restraint here is a demonstration: a studio that cannot control its own margins cannot control a colour grade.

## Anti-references

- **An AI SaaS product page.** Gradient hero, three feature cards, a badge row, "powered by AI" as the selling point. The category default, and the one this brand is escaping.
- **A dropshipping or growth-hack site.** Countdown urgency, loud badges, walls of testimonials, "as seen in" strips. Cheap signals that would undo the price increase on contact.
- **A stock photo marketplace.** Dense asset grids, filter rails, per-image price tags. The framing the reposition abandons by removing per-photo pricing from marketing surfaces.

## Design Principles

**Show the work before the claim.** The first objection is that AI imagery looks cheap, so finished output has to arrive before any price, promise or explanation does.

**Precision is the proof.** With no testimonials to lean on, control is the credibility: exact numbers, honest constraints, hard edges, a palette that does not wobble. Every loose detail reads as a loose colour grade.

**Never borrow credibility we haven't earned.** No invented testimonials, client names, logos, metrics or results. Figures from the wider industry are labelled as such. Dates are only promised when the capacity gate has cleared them.

**Colour marks the point; the photograph still carries it.** *(Revised Aug 2026 — this
principle used to read "the photograph is the only colour", and the site stopped obeying
it the day the palette moved to the OPP-derived scheme. A principle the tree contradicts
is worse than no principle, so it is restated rather than quietly ignored.)*

The interface is near-black so the photography stays the most saturated thing on the
screen — that part is unchanged and is why photographic tiles are never given a coloured
fill. What changed is that colour is now allowed to do one job: marking the single thing
in a section that must be read. One vivid tile per grid, the accent word in a heading,
the figures in the stat row. Applied to everything it would be applied to nothing, and
the photography would be competing with the furniture.

**Say the number.** Prices, deadlines, what is included and what is not — stated in the open, in body copy, not deferred to a quote.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.2 AA. Body text at least 4.5:1 against its background, large text at least 3:1, placeholder text held to the same 4.5:1 as body. Every animation ships a `prefers-reduced-motion` alternative — the chrome hero field freezes rather than disappears — and `prefers-reduced-data` is honoured the same way. Reveal animations enhance an already-visible default and never gate visibility. Interactive targets carry a visible square focus ring drawn with `outline` and `outline-offset`. Bilingual EN/NL throughout, with `hreflang` alternates on every route.
